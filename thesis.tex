\chapter{Introduction}
% This chapter sets the stage, explaining why your project matters and what you intend to achieve.
In a time of rapid global digitalization and a great increase in the need to process data, the demand for high throughput and reliable communication systems keeps growing.
With the expansion of data rates, the need for security also increases. 
Classic \ac{RF} systems, although widely used, might not satisfy the needs of all customers, due to the high traffic on unlicensed \ac{ISM} bands and possible unauthorized listening.

However, there might be a situation where the fiber is also too expensive or simply too difficult to install.

\ac{FSO} communication might meet the requirements that RF lacks and gather the most demanding users. 
Using a precisely focused light beam, this solution can exceed RF solutions in terms of throughput, but also provides great security, by higher directivity and  difficulty of hijacking the signal. Those characteristics make FSO systems a highly competitive answer to the need for private high throughput connections\cite{trichili2020roadmap}. 

FSO is still being improved, despite having many advanced solutions, which are thoroughly described in academic papers, for exmaple Deep Space Optical Communications (DSOC), that allows for over 2AU distance and over 200Mbs throughput transmission \cite{driscoll2020deep}. 
On the other hand there are also new solutions, that are dedicated for ground to ground applications, that utilize use of off-shelf components like SFP modules \cite{selfhealacademic}, \cite{mustafa2016fso}, \cite{abadi2019implementation}.
\section{Background}
% Brief introduction to Free Space Optical (FSO) communication and its advantages (high speed, license-free).
%Free space optical communication bases on transmitting data through the air using light source, typically a laser, which is detected on the other end by the photodiode.
%Such communication have planty of advantages, such as high data rates, license-free operation, and immunity to electromagnetic interference. 
%A very important aspect of FSO communication is security of such connection, because the beam is narrow and tapping into it breaks the connection.
%However, it also has some disadvantages, such as sensitivity to atmospheric conditions and the need for precise alignment.

The fundamental requirement of a well functioning free space optical link is maintaining uninterrupted direct line of sight, between transmitter and receiver.
RF systems, on the other hand, diverge and bounce the obstacles or even pass through them and still can reach the destination.
Ideally FSO systems, keep the transmitted beam collimated, which allows to achieve high power density and security of transmission at the expense of need for keeping precise alignment.
In practical deployments, it is a challenging task because of environmental factors such as vibrations, thermal expansion, or atmospheric conditions.
That is why modern FSO systems are required to actively compensate movements of laser beam and its position.


\section{Motivation}
Although FSO systems are well described in scientific papers, developing such devices is a quite complex engineering task.
The main motivation for taking this topic is the need to create an affordable and functional proof of concept transceiver and establish a high throughput link. 
Completing such connection is challenging because it requires knowledge of multiple fields: designing optical system, precise mechanics, electronics, and low level embedded system programming with real time requirements.   

\section{Thesis Objectives}
%Clearly state your goal to design and develop a stable, high-speed FSO Ethernet link with active misalignment compensation.
The goal of this thesis is to design and develop a stable, high-speed FSO Ethernet link with active misalignment compensation.
This will be achieved by using  readily available SFP modules and a custom-built mechanical system for beam steering, controlled by a microcontroller that reads the \ac{DDM} data from the SFP module to maintain optimal alignment.
The implemented link will be used as a platform to analyze practical issues induced by environmental factors. 

Ultimately, this thesis demonstrates that the integration of \ac{COTS} electronic components with custom 3D-printed mechanics provides a viable and highly adaptable framework for developing operational free-space optical (FSO) communication links.

\section{Principles of FSO Communication}
%How optical data links work and their general architecture.

Generally, a FSO link is a set of two components: a transmitter and a receiver. 
The transmitter sends data signals free space using modulated optical radiation. 
It consists of radiating element, which is usually a laser diode, a laser modulator, and a set of optics responsible for creating a beam \cite{majumdar2014fundamentals}.

Modulation is essential in an optical transmitter. 
It allows data to be embedded into a light beam that then travels a significant distance to the receiver.
The easiest way of modulating an optical signal is using direct modulation - changing an injection current of a laser diode, which affects the output optical power. 
In this way, on-off binary modulation can be achieved, more widely known as \ac{OOK}\cite{hui2025introduction}.
OOK simply trun on the light source to transmit a logic "one" and turns it off to transmit a "zero". 
There are two types of OOK which are \ac{NRZ} and \ac{RZ}. 
RZ version of the OOK simply encodes logic "one" as high amplitude followed by low amplitude, where NRZ does not return to low value, as shown in the figure \ref{fig:mod}. 
RZ has the advantage of higher sensitivity, but both modulations might lead to clock desynchronization during multiple zeros transmitted in a row \cite{henniger2010introduction}. OOK is proven to be working in FSO systems as it is used in other implementations \cite{yang2016free}.

\end{figure}


In order to increase the throughput of the network, \ac{WDM} is used. Instead of using one wavelength of the light to send data, multiple wavelengths are sent in the same optical channel as shown in figure \ref{fig:wdm1}. Different colors of the light do not affect each other and thanks to that properties, the overall throughput can be multiplied up to tens of Terabits \cite{arumugam2001optical}.

\end{figure}

Depending on how dense the wavelength channels are "packed" in the spectrum, this technology is called CWDM or DWDM as shown in figure \ref{fig:wdm2}.

 \begin{figure}[h!]

The modulated and multiplexed light beam now enters an optical system that often consists of a telescope or a collimator, which makes the light beam capable of traveling long distances.
This signal needs to travel through an open space to the receiver.
Line of sight between the transmitter and receiver is required.
There are many factors that influence the path of the light, some directly like weather conditions and others indirectly such as vibrations, wind, or thermal expansion.
Taking these into account, tracking systems must be applied\cite{bloom2002physics}, \cite{khalighi2014survey}.

When the beam reaches it's destination, it enters the optic systems, which focuses it at the photodiode, which converts light into an electrical signal.
Then the signal is demodulated and the data is received at the other end of the link.

\section{Impact of envirometnal factors on light propagation}
The design and implementation of FSO systems require a solid background on the environmental factors that impact light propagation. Key examples include \cite{malik2015free}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Physical obstructions: Temporary block of the \ac{LOS} can happen when objects, such as birds or debris, pass through the beam path,
  \item Scattering: Light signal can scatter when passes through water droplets. Fog, in particular, is the most significant limiting factor for transmission \cite{khandakar2018experimental},
  \item Atmospheric turbulence: Temperature changes in the air create pockets with different refractive indices. Passing through these regions can cause the beam to change it direction,
  \item Atmospheric attenuation: Signal power is reduced over distance over a constant 0.225dBm/km \cite{abd2013effect},
  \item Physical misalignment: Temperature changes can cause thermal expansion or contraction in the mounting structures, leading to misalignment.
\end{itemize}
\section{SFP Modules and Digital Diagnostic Monitoring (DDM)}
The \ac{SFP} modules are standard piece of equipment that can be bought at an affordable price. 
These are compact transceivers that are used in telecommunications, which translates differential electrical signal into the modulated optical signal.

Such modules inside are shown in the figure \ref{fig:sfp}. The main elements are Laser Diode transmitter optical sub-assembly (TOSA) - used for transmitting data, and the receiving part uses receiver optical sub-assembly (ROSA) with PIN diode and transimpedance amplifier. In bidirectional one channel SFP both of those elements are replaced with BOSA (Bi-Directional Optical Sub-Assembly), which is combination of both. In addition to those main optical elements, electrical elements are also included in the PCBA, an amplifier used to boost the power of the received signal, Laser Diode Driver which directly modulates the laser diode. On the other side of the PCB, that can't be seen on the following picture is Digital Diagnostic IC for DDM implementation.

 \begin{figure}[h!]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=1\textwidth]{figures/sfp.png}
  \caption[short]{Dual channel SFP, Source: \cite{linovision_2024_sfp}}
  \label{fig:sfp}
\end{figure}

It is intended to be used in fiber cables that extend tens or even hundreds of kilometers.
SFP modules have various type of connectors, like RJ45 for electrical, LC and SC for optical. 
As written before, these modules are transceivers, allowing for full duplex communication. 
It is realized by two physical optic channels or one optic channel using two different wavelengths (eg. 1550nm Rx/1330nm Tx).

Typically SFP uses wavelengths of telecommunication windows, which are 1st window - 850nm, 2nd window - 1310nm and 3rd window 1550nm. 
These wavelengths are perfect for fiber communication because of the low transmission loss in the fibers \cite{agrawal2012fiber}.

The SFP modules are equipped with \ac{DDM}, which is management technology that allows users to monitor in real time a few transmission parameters.
Those parameters are: output optical power, received power, temperature, laser current, and transceiver supply voltage.

The mentioned characteristics make an SFP modules a great candidate for the base element of the FSO transceiver.
It drives the laser, handles the modulation, and also implement the receiving path.
Different connectors and wavelenghts configurations allow for different designs of the transceiver. 
DDM allows for real time data harvesting, which is essential for a tracking system.


\section{Existing Beam Steering Techniques}
FSO link during its existence might destabilize, which can lead to loss of received power, reduction of throughput, and in the worst case total loss of connection.
To limit the impact of environmental factors that destroy the connection, providers of FSO links are implementing beam steering into their links.
On top of that alignment of long range link, without any beam steering is a tedious task, as it would require perfect alignment of two nodes.
Typical application of FSO link environment conditions are changing, such transceivers are usually placed outdoors and are affected by changing thermal expansion of the materials and structures they are placed on \cite{ruadulescu2022thermal}. In such a case, the perfect kinematic mount, which is perfectly aligned, can change an angle a little bit which can lead to major shifts over the deployment distance.
At first glance it seems to be unnecessary, because in lab conditions the link might work correctly, but the deployment of a long range link is a hard reality check.


The easiest way to provide beam steering into the design is to create a mechanical structure capable of rotating the whole transceiver. 
Using such mechanism can provide big coverage and it is a great solution when transceivers are placed on mobile platforms, for example UAV drone.

The next possible solution is to implement translation into the optical system.
Movement of the lens or the mirror used to launch the beam allows for change of angle in which the beam exits the transceiver.

Lastly, the translation of optical element could be swapped with a tilt of the lens or the mirror, but tilting might introduce an increased level of difficulty, of understanding and modeling such movement.

\section{Mechanical and Optical Design}
%Details of the 3D-printed structure, lens positioning, and the integration of stepper motors for pan/tilt or lens shifting.
The crucial part of the transceiver is the optical and mechanical design. 
In this particular case it is embedded together and movement provided by mechanics changes the position of the light source to the lens.
It changes the angle in which the the light exists the transceiver, allowing beam steering.

In case, when light source is placed in the focal length - the light beam will be collimated. 
Approximately if we move it in parallel to the lens, the beam will be almost collimated and will exit the lens at the angle.
If there is a second light beam coming into the transceiver at the same angle as the exiting beam, it will focus in the same place as the light source.
Due to this characteristic, this beam steering technique can work, allowing transceivers to communicate when they are shifting from ideal alignment, as shown in \ref{fig4:2_lenses}.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering

To achieve such beam steering, proper mechanical system has been created that allows for 3 axis movement of the SFP module.
First axis, let's call it x is perpendicular to the lens and translation alongside this axis change the distance of SFP to the lens.
Another two axes, y and z are parallel to the lens and moving the source in those direction steers the beam, as shown in the figure \ref{fig5:axes}. 

\begin{figure}[H]

The mechanical structure allowing movements alongside those axes has been based on lead screws and anti-backlash nut's rotated by stepper motors.
The used screw has 1mm pitch, which means that one full rotation of the screw will move the element by 1mm.
Besides the lead screw, nuts and bearings, the rest of the mechanics was design for 3D printing.
Although 3D printing does not give best results of the mechanical structure (Low stiffnes, thermal properties and precision), it is great for rapid development and prototyping.

The 3D printed structure also includes guides that the elements move on.

It consists of couple elements connected by GPIO's or communication protocols.

The firmware, which is run by the STM32 microcontroller, is a Non-Preemptive, 
Polling-Driven Single-Tasking Architecture, which calls multiple Finite State Machines (FSM).
 The tasks it runs are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Movement Handler - it drives the motors and checks the end switches,

  \item Serial Comms - checks the UART communication, from here FSM's can be toggled from sleep to run,
  \item DDM Readout - reads the DDM data with given period,
  \item Optimize FSM - algorithm that tries to optimize the received power for given distance from the lens,

\section{Connection Seeking Algorithm (Spiral Search)}

After placing the transceivers so that they are aimed at each other, it is demanding task to achieve perfect aligment.
 It is because of imperfections of the mechanical design, or even media converter itself, the SFP module can be a little bit rotated.
It can be calibrated coarsely, but in the end a search algorithm had to be developed.

The algorithm applied is the Square Spiral Search Pattern Method. 
Due to high directivity and an imperfect homing sequence, the spatial search algorithm must be applied to connect both transceviers.
The flow chart has been presented in the figure \ref{Fig11:Spiral search}. The algorithm has an asynchronous stop condition. 
The lower the dx the denser the search. 
That dependency allows for using the search in different scenarios,
like in case the connection was lost dense version will be appropriate.
During link set up, rather coarse version is suggested, because the field of search is much bigger.

\section{Power aligment algorithm}
After running a spiral search, the received power is at a low, nonoptimal power level.
The purpose of Optimizing Algorithm is to find a maximum received power point, by controlling the Y/Z axes.
In this moment the continuous connection isn't required, since it's still running during link set up.

The proposed algorithm is a bounded linear search, the flow chart is shown in figure \ref{Fig11:linear search}.
It sweeps three times to ensure that it approaches the peak from both sides and in case it starts going in wrong direction.
The change direction condition is limited to the quater of current maxPow, to make the algorithm a than a full sweep.
The 50 ms delay between moving and currentPow readout lets the mechanical vibrations settle to make the readout undistorted.
Firstly, such an algorithm should be applied to one axis and then another \cite{arora2014two}.

\section{Misalignment Compensation}
%How the software specifically differentiates between standard signal fluctuation and physical drift caused by thermal expansion or physical transceiver head movement.
There are a few more nuances in the active compensation.
Firstly, the reading from the sensor might fluctuate and can be affected by high frequency changes.

To get rid of unwanted readings, some sort of low pass filter must be applied.
The LPF of choice in this case was \ac{EMA}.
It is an algorithm that does not require memory for saving the samples, in comparison to other moving averages like Simple Moving Average (SMA) or Weighted Moving Average (WMA) \cite{lotysh2023comparison}.
The equation for an EMA is:
\begin{equation}
  y_i = \alpha * x_i + (1 - \alpha) * y_i
\end{equation}
where y is the output, x is the input and alpha is a constant in the range [0, 1].


Another point is that the link is established by two transceivers. 
Running algorithms without communication between 2 ends would lead to strange results and decalibration.
To make it convenient, RF communication was introduced into the design.


The basic handshake is introduced, as can be seen in \ref{Fig12:Exec_flow}.
\begin{figure}[h!]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.6\textwidth]{figures/Execution_diagram.png}
  \caption[short]{Execution flow}
  \label{Fig12:Exec_flow}
\end{figure}

Such a sequence should guaranty good execution of hill climb algorithms in both transceivers of a link.
The order is that the transceiver initializing the compensation dispatches Control Algorithm on the second transceiver first 
ensures that no algorithm will be run when there is no working connection.

\section{Discussion}
%An honest evaluation of your system's stability, limitations of the 3D-printed mechanics, and the speed bottleneck of polling DDM data.
In the baseline performance, two cases were observed, conditions were received power stayed stable and one with significant drop of received power. Although power metrics stayed in reasonable range allowing for full throughput, in longer range links the drift could destabilize the link.

The results of Active Compensation in comparison to baseline performance show positive results - the received power metric was controlled at kept at good level.
There are some nuances that can be taken into account while improving the overall performance.
Currently, the transceiver is optimizing the received power of itself, but refined verison would be optimizing the sum of both received powers.
The biggest bottleneck of the system is the DDM polling data frequency, 15ms makes a transceiver appropriate for not moving applications.
If there was an intention to apply this communication in drone communication to vehicle communication, the update would be too slow and would require other input data to base the compensation. 
Also, such application would require a much higher range of motion. The current mechanics could be a nice base for "fine" steering in such a system, where "coarse" steering would be a rotation of the base of transceiver.

Overall, the 3D printed mechanics and the steppers motors give nice stability for "last mile" applications, allowing for high throughput secure connections where it is impossible or too expensive to lay fiber. 
This is also a pretty good base, which could be extended for more advanced application, which would run real time tracking, but that would require to swap current SFP module for custom electronics, allowing for faster data harvest.

The project was carried out successfully according to the objective of the thesis.

\section{Future Work}
%What could be improved in future iterations? (e.g., faster motors, different optics, machine learning for predictive tracking).

The FSO transceiver and the link are tasks that can be performed in various ways, depending on the needed application.
The transceiver described by this document aims to be applied in "Last mile" scenarios.

In such a use case used components, such as SFP modules, are sufficient to connecting two buildings together. 
Although the low data refresh in DDM technology bottlenecks the possibilities of compensating different environmental factors.
With current application the link is capable of reducing the slow paced changes, like thermal expansion of the buildings and the device itself.
In higher distances this might start to be an issue where those fast paced vibrations or wind swings might lead to big packet losses.
So the first improvement to make such transceiver greater, would be obtaining its own circuit of receiver and transmitter, which would allow for faster readouts of received power.

Another thing that could be upgraded are mechanics; more precisely, the 3d printed guides would be changed with proper precise guide rail, which would eliminate the loose movements and decrease the friction.
Current movements of the transceiver are also far too long in tens of centimeters, limited to one centimeter range by the end switches. 
Limiting it by design will reduce the amount of materials used in production, including filament and lead screw. 
The production time would also be reduced by shorter 3D print times. 
The last thing regarding the mechanics would be the sealed waterproof casing of the transceiver, which would allow for proper application.

The last thing that would improve the device would be beacon light source, which would allow for link set up without human interaction. 
Such light paired with couple photodiodes would make the setup faster but also would open the new possibilities of automatic link recovery in case of total link degradation.

In the end, it is a Proof of Concept device that is shown to fulfill the objectives. Previously mentioned improvements might be used to improve this device in real world applications.
